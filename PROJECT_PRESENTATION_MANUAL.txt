1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE MISSION
====================================================================================================

Traditional photo restoration methods rely on manual digital retouching (Photoshop) or simple
heuristic filters (median filters, bilateral filtering, unsharp masking). These conventional techniques
drastically fail when applied to real vintage photographs because historical images suffer from 
COMPOUNDED DEGRADATIONS:
  1. Structured Defects   : Deep physical scratches, fold cracks, water stains, paper tears, dust.
  2. Unstructured Defects : Sepia tone color fading, loss of contrast, sensor/chemical film noise, blur.
  3. Identity Degradation : Blurred facial features, loss of eye sharpness, lips, teeth, and hair texture.

Standard deep learning frameworks (like CycleGAN or Pix2Pix) fail when trained on synthetic degradations
because real-world vintage photos have a massive "domain gap": synthetic scratches do not look like
real 80-year-old chemical paper cracks.

OUR SOLUTION:
We implement a specialized, four-stage hybrid deep generative framework that:
  - Disentangles the restoration into Global Processing and Local Face Processing.
  - Utilizes Triplet Domain Translation via two distinct Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) coupled by
    a Latent Space Mapping Network.
  - Employs a dedicated UNet scratch segmentation network paired with Non-Local Attention modules
    to borrow contextual texture from undamaged areas to fill scratches.
  - Utilizes 68-point facial landmark detection (dlib) to crop, align, and super-resolve faces using
    a SPADE (Spatially-Adaptive Denormalization) generative adversarial network.
  - Re-integrates the enhanced faces into the restored background using histogram matching and
    feathered alpha-blending for seamless, artifact-free restoration.
  - Accompanied by an interactive Graphical User Interface (GUI) and quantitative evaluation metrics
    (MSE, PSNR, SSIM).

4. HOW IT WORKS: DEEP LEARNING & THEORETICAL UNDERPINNINGS
====================================================================================================

4.1 The Compounded Degradation Problem & The Domain Gap
-------------------------------------------------------
Vintage photos suffer from two fundamentally different types of degradations:
  - Structured Degradation: Localized, high-frequency physical cuts, fold tears, surface abrasions.
  - Unstructured Degradation: Global sepia color shifts, chemical sensor noise, blur, contrast fading.

If you train a conventional GAN (e.g. CycleGAN) on synthetic scratches, it will fail on real old photos
because synthetic artifacts follow an artificial mathematical distribution, whereas real vintage photos
have aged paper grain, fading chemistry, and irregular physical fissures. This is known as the DOMAIN GAP.

4.2 Triplet Domain Translation & Dual VAE Latent Mapping
--------------------------------------------------------
To solve the domain gap, the project introduces TRIPLET DOMAIN TRANSLATION:
  - Domain R: Real old degraded photographs (Unpaired, unlabeled).
  - Domain X: Synthetic degraded photographs (Generated by adding synthetic noise/scratches to clean photos).
  - Domain Y: Ground-truth clean modern photographs.

Instead of translating directly in image pixel space (which causes hallucinations and artifacting),
we use two Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):
  1. VAE_A (Degraded Domain Autoencoder):
     Takes a degraded photo (Domain R or X) and encodes it into a compact, continuous latent space Z_X.
     L_VAE_A = Reconstruction_Loss(x, x_hat) + KL_Divergence(q(z|x) || p(z))
  2. VAE_B (Clean Domain Autoencoder):
     Takes a clean photo (Domain Y) and encodes it into a clean latent space Z_Y.
  3. Latent Space Mapping Network M:
     A residual convolutional network that translates between the two latent spaces:
     z_y_pred = M(z_x)
     Because translation occurs in low-dimensional, compact latent space rather than raw RGB pixels,
     the network maps abstract semantic content without being misled by surface-level chemical noise!
  4. VAE_B Decoder:
     Decodes z_y_pred directly into a crisp, restored RGB image.

4.3 Scratch Detection via Specialized UNet & Non-Local Attention
----------------------------------------------------------------
Scratches are physical missing data. The system handles them in two steps:
  Step 1: Detection Network (`Global/detection.py`)
    A modified UNet with antialiasing filters and synchronized batch normalization scans the photo
    and generates a probability map thresholded at 0.5 to produce a binary defect mask M.
    White pixels (1) = Scratch/Defect; Black pixels (0) = Intact photographic texture.

  Step 2: Non-Local Attention Inpainting (`Global/test.py` - Setting_42)
    Standard convolutions only look at local neighboring pixels. If a scratch is wide, local pixels
    are also corrupted!
    Non-local attention computes a pairwise affinity matrix across the ENTIRE image:
      Affinity(i, j) = softmax( (theta(x_i))^T * phi(x_j) / sqrt(d) )
    The corrupted feature at scratch position i is reconstructed by computing a weighted sum of ALL
    uncorrupted features j across the entire image. If a coat pattern or wallpaper is scratched,
    the model borrows texture from distant uncorrupted portions of the same coat or wallpaper!

4.4 68-Point Facial Landmark Detection & Geometric Alignment
------------------------------------------------------------
Faces are the emotional center of historical photos, and human vision is hyper-sensitive to facial
distortions.
  - The system loads `shape_predictor_68_face_landmarks.dat` via `dlib`.
  - It detects 68 canonical facial coordinates: jawline (0-16), eyebrows (17-26), nose bridge & tip
    (27-35), eyes (36-47), and lips (48-67).
  - From these coordinates, 5 key anchor points are extracted:
      - Left eye center, Right eye center, Nose tip, Left mouth corner, Right mouth corner.
  - A Similarity Transform (rotation + isotropic scale + translation) maps these 5 points to standard
    canonical face coordinates, producing a square crop (256x256 or 512x512) centered precisely on the face.

4.5 Progressive Generative Face Enhancement (SPADE)
---------------------------------------------------
Restoring heavily blurred vintage faces requires generative detail synthesis.
  - A progressive GAN generator equipped with SPADE (Spatially-Adaptive Denormalization) takes the
    degraded crop and reconstructs high-frequency details.
  - SPADE modulates feature activations using learned scale and bias conditioned on facial semantics:
      gamma(y) * (h - mu) / sigma + beta(y)
  - This guarantees that fine anatomical structures (pupils, eyelashes, hair follicles, skin texture)
    are synthesized crisply without morphing the person's identity.

4.6 Inverse Warping, Color Histogram Matching & Feathered Blending
------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the face is enhanced, it cannot simply be pasted back, otherwise it looks like an unnatural sticker.
  1. Histogram Matching:
     Computes the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of the restored face and source face:
       lookup_table[src_pixel] = ref_pixel
     Matches lighting, sepia tone, and contrast so the face matches the global photograph's ambiance.
  2. Inverse Similarity Transform:
     Inverts the transformation matrix (T_inv = T^-1) and warps the enhanced face back to the exact
     original head orientation and tilt.
  3. Gaussian Blurred Alpha Mask Feathering:
     Generates an elliptical boundary mask softened with Gaussian blur. Pixels at the perimeter
     smoothly transition between the restored face and the global background:
       Final_Pixel = Alpha * Enhanced_Face + (1 - Alpha) * Global_Background

5.1 run.py (The Master Orchestration Engine)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: `Bringing-Old-Photos-Back-to-Life/run.py`
Purpose : The central pipeline driver. Takes input arguments, resolves directories, and sequences
          Stages 1 through 4 via automated subprocess execution.

Key Code Highlights:
  - Command Line Arguments (Lines 19-33):
      --input_folder     : Directory containing raw old photographs.
      --output_folder    : Target directory for results.
      --GPU              : GPU device ID (e.g. "0") or "-1" for CPU execution.
      --with_scratch     : Boolean flag indicating if scratch detection UNet should run.
      --HR               : High-Resolution mode (uses 512x512 models and patch attention).

  - Stage 1 Execution (Lines 45-106):
      If `--with_scratch` is active, it first executes `Global/detection.py` to create `masks/mask`,
      then invokes `Global/test.py --Scratch_and_Quality_restore` passing both input image and mask.
      If `--with_scratch` is not active, it directly runs `Global/test.py --Quality_restore`.
      Copies Stage 1 output into `final_output` as a fallback in case the photo has no faces.

  - Stage 2 Execution (Lines 110-128):
      Changes directory to `Face_Detection/` and runs `detect_all_dlib.py`.
      Crops and aligns detected faces into `stage_2_detection_output`.

  - Stage 3 Execution (Lines 130-170):
      Changes directory to `Face_Enhancement/` and runs `test_face.py`.
      Loads checkpoint `Setting_9_epoch_100` (or `FaceSR_512` in HR mode) and outputs enhanced
      faces into `stage_3_face_output/each_img`.

  - Stage 4 Execution (Lines 172-200):
      Changes directory to `Face_Detection/` and runs `align_warp_back_multiple_dlib.py`.
      Combines `stage_1_restore_output` and `stage_3_face_output` into `final_output`.

5.3 Global/detection.py (Scratch Segmentation UNet)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: `Bringing-Old-Photos-Back-to-Life/Global/detection.py`
Purpose : Segments physical scratches and cracks into binary masks.

Key Functions & Components:
  - `data_transforms(img, full_size, method=Image.BICUBIC)` (Lines 25-49):

  - UNet Model Initialization (Lines 76-89):
      Instantiates `networks.UNet(in_channels=1, out_channels=1, depth=4, conv_num=2, wf=6, antialiasing=True)`.
      Operates on grayscale luminance channel where scratch contrast is sharpest.
  - Pretrained Weights Loading (Lines 91-94):
      Loads `checkpoints/detection/FT_Epoch_latest.pt` containing fine-tuned scratch detection weights.
  - Thresholding & Mask Generation (Lines 140-165):
      Outputs sigmoid probability map. Values > 0.5 are classified as scratches.
      Saves the binary mask as `mask.png` and creates masked input `input.png`.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: `Bringing-Old-Photos-Back-to-Life/Face_Detection/align_warp_back_multiple_dlib.py`

Key Functions & Components:
  - `calculate_cdf(histogram)` & `calculate_lookup(src_cdf, ref_cdf)` (Lines 26-60):
      Computes the Cumulative Distribution Function for source and reference image histograms to

      Inverts the affine matrix, calculates an elliptical Gaussian-blurred mask, and performs
      alpha-compositing:
        Output = Mask * Warped_Face + (1 - Mask) * Global_Photo.

5.8 Evaluation.ipynb (Benchmarking: MSE, PSNR, SSIM & Synthetic Degradation)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: `Bringing-Old-Photos-Back-to-Life/Evaluation.ipynb`
Purpose : Scientific benchmarking and objective quantitative performance evaluation.

Key Cells & Logic:
  - Section 1: Definition of Image Quality Metrics:
      MSE  : Pixel-level error. Formula: (1/N) * sum((Y_true - Y_pred)^2). Lower is better.
      PSNR : Signal-to-noise ratio in decibels. Formula: 10 * log10(MAX^2 / MSE). Higher is better.
      SSIM : Structural similarity (luminance, contrast, structural correlation). Range [-1, 1].
             Higher (closer to 1.0) is better.
  - Section 2: `add_synthetic_scratches(image_path, output_path)`:
      Generates controlled benchmark test images by injecting random white linear scratch artifacts
      and Gaussian noise (mean=0, sigma=15) into a clean photograph.
  - Section 3: Model Inference Execution:
      Calls `run.py` to restore the synthetically degraded image.
  - Section 4: Quantitative Scoring & Visualization:
      Computes baseline error (Degraded vs Clean) vs. model performance (Restored vs Clean).
      Plots side-by-side visual comparisons confirming dramatic PSNR and SSIM improvements.

8. VIVA VOCE / TEACHER DEFENSE QUESTIONS & BULLETPROOF ANSWERS
====================================================================================================

Q1: Why can't we just use standard CycleGAN or Pix2Pix for old photo restoration?
A1: Standard CycleGAN and Pix2Pix operate directly in pixel space. Because old photos suffer from
    complex, mixed degradations (film grain, fading, scratches), a direct pixel-to-pixel network
    overfits to synthetic training noise and fails to generalize to real vintage photos (the domain gap).
    Our project uses Triplet Domain Translation via two separate VAEs. By mapping the degraded image
    into a continuous, smooth latent space Z_X and translating to a clean latent space Z_Y, the model
    restores semantic features without hallucinating surface-level artifacts.

Q2: Why do you have a separate pipeline for faces? Why not restore everything together?
A2: Human visual perception is exceptionally sensitive to faces. Even minor distortions or blurring
    around the eyes, mouth, or nose appear unnatural ("uncanny valley"). A global restoration network
    prioritizes the large image background and tends to over-smooth facial details. By isolating faces
    using dlib 68-point landmarks, standardizing their geometry, and running them through a dedicated
    SPADE progressive generator trained on high-res faces, we achieve photorealistic facial sharpness
    while preserving the person's true identity.

Q3: How does the Non-Local Attention mechanism work for scratch removal?
A3: Standard convolutional layers have small local receptive fields (e.g. 3x3 or 5x5 filters). If a
    scratch covers a 20-pixel wide patch, all local pixels are damaged, so local convolution has no clean
    context to work with. Non-Local Attention calculates pairwise affinity between the damaged scratch
    patch and EVERY other patch in the entire image. It allows the network to borrow matching textures
    from undamaged regions far away (such as another part of the person's suit or background wallpaper)
    to fill the scratch seamlessly.

Q4: What metrics did you use to evaluate the restoration quality?
A4: Because real vintage photos have no ground truth (we don't have the original pristine photo from
    1920), we evaluated the model using a full-reference synthetic testbed in `Evaluation.ipynb`:
      1. MSE (Mean Squared Error): Measures raw pixel intensity discrepancy. Lower is better.
      2. PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio): Logarithmic ratio of maximum signal power to noise power
         in decibels (dB). Higher is better.
      3. SSIM (Structural Similarity Index): Measures perceptual structural, luminance, and contrast
         fidelity between 0 and 1. Higher is better.
    Our restored outputs show significant drops in MSE and marked increases in both PSNR and SSIM.

Q5: What is the purpose of Histogram Matching in Stage 4?
A5: When the face crop is enhanced by the generative network in Stage 3, the output face might have a
    slightly different color balance or exposure compared to the aged sepia tone of the original photo.
    Histogram matching computes the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of both regions and normalizes
    the color distribution of the enhanced face to match the surrounding vintage photograph, preventing
    the face from looking like a pasted sticker.
